# Market research: tools similar to World Network

Researched 2026-11-03. World Network = a personal contact network on a world map, with Google/CSV/LinkedIn
import, two-way Google sync, tags/communities, country and flag views, history, and planned
mobility, gamification and social phases.

No single product does all of that. The competition sits in four groups:

1. **Personal CRMs** (relationship upkeep; a map is a side feature or absent)
2. **Friend-map apps** (where people live; mostly small, new, iOS-only)
3. **Live location / travel-social** (where people are right now, or where they travel)
4. **"Countries visited" trackers** (the gamification side)

## How to read the numbers

Download and active-user counts are not public for almost any of these apps. What I could see:
company claims (marked "claims"), App Store / Google Play rating counts (a rough proxy: apps usually
get one rating per few hundred to a few thousand installs), and review-site counts. Where I could not
confirm a launch date it says "not verified". Pricing comes from vendor pages where I could reach
them, otherwise from aggregators, which often disagree.

## What they charge for

- **Contact count caps** are the most common paywall: 10 (UpHabit), 15 (FriendMapp), 25 (YourPond),
  1,000 recent contacts (Mesh).
- **Subscription band:** $10-14/mo or about $100/yr for personal CRMs. Friend-map apps are cheaper
  ($20/yr, $1.99-3.99 tiers) or one-time ($6.99, $79.99 lifetime).
- **Advanced views behind Pro:** YourPond puts connection clusters in Pro; FriendMapp puts the Globe in Pro.
- **AI features:** enrichment, natural-language entry, conversation starters, reminders.
- **Physical keepsakes:** Polarsteps' printed books make more money than its subscription.
- **Teams/shared contacts:** $49/seat (Mesh), $24-60/seat (folk).
- **Lifetime deals** are used to attract early adopters (FriendMapp, Nomad List).

## What users say

### Praise
- Automatic enrichment that fills in data you would otherwise type (Mesh: "does it better than most").
- Alerts about relationships going cold (Mesh reviewers kept coming back to this).
- Clean, playful design and a map that is the product, not a tab (Zenly, Bump).
- Privacy: on-device, no ads, no data sales, not used to train AI (Pinfrnz, Sidewalk, Persodex, YourPond, maap).
- A focus on relationships, not sales pipelines (Dex reviewers).

### Complaints
- **Manual data entry kills it.** The repeated story is that people add a few contacts and quit within
  about two weeks. One Reddit comment: "Overcomplicated UI + constant manual data entry... kills productivity".
- **Bugs and sync failures:** Mesh notes not syncing to mobile, iMessage import failing with no retry,
  "super buggy and unfocused on its core promise"; Dex "broken at every turn" / buggy onboarding.
- **Duplicates:** Mesh has no automatic dedupe; folk only catches exact first+last name matches.
- **Geocoding gaps:** Pinfrnz can't find Detroit or smaller cities; Wheriz imported only some contacts.
- **Map usability:** Dex users asked for a list of the contacts shown in the current map area, and the
  map needs a refresh after you edit a location.
- **Thin organisation tools:** Mesh's tags, groups and lists are weak; its mobile workflows are clunky.
- **Platform gaps:** most friend-map apps are iOS-only; Mesh has no Android; Dex's map is web-only.
- **Empty social features:** Nomad List is only active in big cities; Bump's scratch maps can't be seen
  by friends yet.
- **Shutdown risk:** Dopplr and Zenly users lost products they loved.

### Wishes
- See all contacts on a global map (Dex's original request; a Quantified Self forum thread; YourPond's
  own pitch says Apple/Google Contacts have "no map view").
- "Who do I know in the city I'm visiting?" with alerts on arrival (Gullu, Persodex, Dopplr's old core).
- Track when people move: Based / Visiting / Moved status and per-contact location history (Pinfrnz,
  YourPond). Moves are currently scattered across Instagram and LinkedIn posts and easily forgotten.
- A network graph showing how contacts connect, such as a friend and their spouse (Obsidian Friend
  Tracker users, a self-hosted CRM thread, YourPond's "Ripples").
- Import past trips, check-ins, and log places driven through (Bump, been reviewers).
- Location precision you control, from city to country (Nomad's Map, sparrow).

## Where World Network already stands out

- **Cross-platform:** PWA on iOS, Android and web, while most map competitors are iOS-only.
- **Real geocoding coverage:** GeoNames cities15000/cities500 plus a manual picker, which is exactly
  what Pinfrnz users complain is missing.
- **Two-way Google sync** with delete propagation and phone/link matching. Few personal CRMs sync both
  ways, and dedupe is a named complaint elsewhere.
- **LinkedIn CSV import plus a map.** Nobody currently maps LinkedIn connections.
- **Country choropleth and flag-colour dots:** no competitor found has these views.
- **Data security story** (RLS, no anon grants, 30-day deletion grace) that you can market as privacy-first.

## Inspiration: features worth considering, ranked by evidence

1. **Trip overlap / "who's in X"**: enter an upcoming trip, see who lives there or will be there, and
   get notified. This was Dopplr's core, Gullu and Persodex sell it now, and nobody owns it. Fits your
   Mobility phase.
2. **Location status and history per contact** (Based / Visiting / Moved, with dates): this answers
   the "people move and I forget" wish. Your mobility and history tables are close to this already.
3. **Stay-in-touch layer**: last contacted, a "going cold" nudge, birthdays from Google `details`. This
   is the most-praised CRM feature and World Network has nothing like it yet.
4. **Low-effort data entry**: natural-language or voice "Anna moved to Lisbon" (YourPond). Also
   suggest a city for LinkedIn contacts from company/position, or ask once in bulk, since the
   LinkedIn export has no location.
5. **List of contacts in the current map area**: Dex users asked for it directly.
6. **Gamification**: countries/cities "collected" through your network, "% of the world you know
   someone in", and Passport-style stamps (been, maap, YourPond, Bump). This matches your planned
   stats phase.
7. **Connection graph** (who knows whom, households, communities as clusters).
8. **Privacy controls you can show**: city-only precision, export everything, no AI training. Users
   weigh these heavily.
9. **Monetisation pattern, if you charge**: free up to roughly 50-100 contacts or with all core
   views, Pro around $5-10/mo for sync, trips and alerts, a lifetime deal for early adopters, and
   possibly a printed "your world" poster or book (the Polarsteps model).
